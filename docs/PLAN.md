# The plan: learning the core, the path, and what the Buddha actually taught

*Working document. What the site has, what it is missing, what the Buddha's own
inventory of his teaching looks like, and how to make all of it learnable rather
than merely listed.*

---

## 1. Where we are

Four boards and sixteen chapters. What they cover well:

| Covered | Where |
|---|---|
| The mechanism — dependent origination as a loop with one cuttable edge | main board, ch 01–07 |
| Five aggregates, six sense bases | ch 04–05 |
| Kamma as intention, merit and demerit, how kamma ripens, the death process | ch 08, kamma board |
| The fine grain — kalāpa, cittakkhaṇa, citta vīthi, the 89 cittas | micro board, cittas board |
| The eightfold path, samatha and vipassanā, the hindrances | ch 10–11 |
| Nibbāna, and the audit of the metaphor | ch 12–13 |

What is thin or missing, in order of how much it matters:

1. **The path is only one-eighth present.** The eightfold path is one of *seven*
   sets the Buddha named as his teaching. The other six — and the fact that they
   are built from the same fourteen qualities reused — is not on the site at all.
2. **No progression.** Nothing shows what changes as someone practises: the four
   stages, the ten fetters, which ones fall at which stage.
3. **No curriculum.** The Buddha gave an explicit *sequence* of training. The site
   presents the path as eight simultaneous things (correct) but never shows the
   order in which it is actually walked (also correct, and missing).
4. **No primary sources.** Everything is explained; nothing is quoted at length.
   A reader finishes without having read a single discourse.
5. **No life.** The person who worked this out, and the night he worked it out,
   are absent. That is the most attractive story in the material and it is
   unused.
6. **No lay life.** Precepts, the six directions, the four sublime states, right
   livelihood — the things most readers will actually use — are barely present.
7. **No meditation instruction.** Chapter 11 says what meditation is for. It
   does not say what to do.
8. **No learning mechanics.** No progress, no self-check, no schedule, no
   spacing. A reader has no way to know what they have absorbed.

## 2. The Buddha's own inventory of what he taught

Near the end of his life (DN 16) he named the teachings to be preserved. Not the
whole canon — a specific list of **thirty-seven things in seven groups**, the
*bodhipakkhiyā dhammā*, the wings to awakening. This is the spine of any honest
"what did he teach."

| Set | Count | Items |
|---|---|---|
| Four foundations of mindfulness · satipaṭṭhāna · සතිපට්ඨාන | 4 | body, feelings, mind, phenomena |
| Four right efforts · sammappadhāna · සම්මප්පධාන | 4 | prevent unarisen unwholesome · abandon arisen unwholesome · arouse unarisen wholesome · maintain arisen wholesome |
| Four bases of power · iddhipāda · ඉද්ධිපාද | 4 | desire-to-do, energy, mind, investigation |
| Five faculties · indriya · ඉන්ද්‍රිය | 5 | confidence, energy, mindfulness, collectedness, wisdom |
| Five powers · bala · බල | 5 | the same five, when they cannot be shaken |
| Seven factors of awakening · bojjhaṅga · බොජ්ඣංග | 7 | mindfulness, investigation, energy, rapture, tranquillity, collectedness, equanimity |
| Noble eightfold path · magga · මග්ග | 8 | view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, collectedness |

**The systems point, and it is a good one:** 37 items, but only **fourteen distinct
qualities**. Mindfulness appears eight times. Energy appears nine times. Wisdom, under
four names, five times. Collectedness four times. The seven sets are not seven
programmes — they are one small set of primitives, arranged for different jobs. The
tradition counted this itself (the commentaries give exactly fourteen).

That is the same move the cittas board makes: the list is generated, not memorised.

### The rest of the core, for completeness

Things he taught that are not in the 37 but are load-bearing:

- **Four noble truths** — the diagnostic frame *(have it)*
- **Dependent origination** — the mechanism *(have it)*
- **Three marks** — the runtime properties *(have it)*
- **Five aggregates, six bases, eighteen elements** — the decompositions *(have two of three)*
- **Kamma and rebirth** *(have it)*
- **The four stages and ten fetters** — the progression *(missing)*
- **The gradual training** — the sequence *(missing)*
- **Nibbāna** *(have it)*
- **The three refuges and the triple gem** *(missing)*
- **Five precepts, and the lay code** *(missing)*
- **Four sublime states** — mettā, karuṇā, muditā, upekkhā *(missing)*
- **The ten perfections** — pāramī, central in the Sri Lankan tradition *(missing)*
- **What he refused to answer, and why** — the ten unanswered questions, the arrow, the raft, the handful of leaves *(missing)*

## 3. The path as he actually laid it out

The eightfold path is a *description*. The **gradual training** (anupubbasikkhā,
DN 2 and many others) is the *procedure* — the order in which it is walked. It is
the single most important thing the site does not have, because it is the answer
to "but what do I actually do."

```
01  hearing the teaching, and confidence that it might be true
02  sīla — restraint in body and speech, so there is nothing to conceal
03  contentment — wanting little, so there is less noise
04  guarding the senses — receiving input without launching the elaboration
05  mindfulness and clear comprehension in every ordinary act
06  a quiet place, and abandoning the five hindrances
07  the four jhānas — collectedness deepening in stages
08  turning the collected mind to insight
09  the three knowledges:
      past lives · how beings pass away and reappear · the ending of the taints
10  release, and the knowledge that it is release
```

Read as a system: **each stage lowers the noise floor for the next**. Ethics is
not the moral entry fee — it is what makes the instrument quiet enough to use. The
site already argues this in chapter 10. The gradual training is where the argument
becomes a build order.

### Progression: the four stages and the ten fetters

A ten-flag register. Stages clear bits.

| Fetter · saṃyojana · සංයෝජන | sotāpanna | sakadāgāmi | anāgāmi | arahant |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 self-view · sakkāya-diṭṭhi | cleared | cleared | cleared | cleared |
| 2 doubt · vicikicchā | cleared | cleared | cleared | cleared |
| 3 clinging to rites · sīlabbata-parāmāsa | cleared | cleared | cleared | cleared |
| 4 sense desire · kāmarāga | | weakened | cleared | cleared |
| 5 ill will · byāpāda | | weakened | cleared | cleared |
| 6 desire for form · rūparāga | | | | cleared |
| 7 desire for the formless · arūparāga | | | | cleared |
| 8 conceit · māna | | | | cleared |
| 9 restlessness · uddhacca | | | | cleared |
| 10 not-seeing · avijjā | | | | cleared |

Note what the first stage actually is: **not** the end of craving. It is the end
of *self-view* — the first fetter is the [anattā](#/marks) argument landing. Which
is why the site's ordering, mechanism before ethics before practice, is the
tradition's ordering too.

## 4. The plan, in phases

Each phase is a board plus a chapter or two, in the house style: one insight per
screen, Pali and Sinhala on everything, everyday examples, honest layer labels, and
something to press.

### Phase 1 — The path, complete  ·  **DONE** ()

**Board: the thirty-seven.** A single graph. Seven sets as seven columns, fourteen
qualities as nodes, edges showing where each quality reappears. Click *sati* and it
lights in eight places. The board's argument: *this is fourteen things, not
thirty-seven, and you already met most of them.*

**Board: the gradual training.** Ten stages as a build order, each one a card
with what it is, why it precedes the next, and what "done enough to move on"
looks like. Interactive: a noise-floor meter that drops as stages complete.

**Board: the register.** The ten fetters as ten switches. Press *sotāpanna* and
three clear. Press *arahant* and all ten do. With the note that stage one is
self-view falling, not craving ending.

Chapters: *The fourteen*, *The order it is walked in*, *What changes, and when*.

### Phase 2 — In his words

**Board: the life.** A timeline, and one deliberate framing: the awakening as
three discoveries in one night, each stated as a finding. Birth, the four sights,
the going forth, six years of the wrong method, the middle way, the night, the
first discourse, forty-five years, the last words. Both date traditions given
(Sri Lankan: 623–543 BCE; modern scholarly: c. 480–400 BCE).

**Sutta reader: twelve discourses.** Each with a three-line summary, the key
passage, the Sinhala title, why it matters, and a link to the full text on
SuttaCentral.

Plus the Dhammapada as a standing reference — the verses everyone already
half-knows.

### Phase 3 — Living it

**Board: the six directions.** Sigālovāda as a hub-and-spoke: parents, teachers,
spouse and children, friends, those who work for you, ascetics — with the mutual
duties on both ends of every spoke. The most practical thing the Buddha said to
laypeople, and almost nobody outside Sri Lanka has heard of it.

**Board: the four sublime states.** Mettā, karuṇā, muditā, upekkhā — as a
widening-radius simulation: self, a friend, a neutral person, a difficult person,
all beings. With their "near enemies" (what looks like each but is not) and "far
enemies".

**The precepts and the perfections.** Five precepts with what each protects. Ten
pāramī with the Sri Lankan emphasis on them explained.

**Meditation lab.** Ānāpānasati's sixteen steps as a guided pipeline in four
tetrads. The hindrances against the jhāna factors as an antidote table — five
problems, five specific counters. Mettā bhāvanā as a script. Everything labelled
*this is instruction, not a substitute for a teacher.*

### Phase 4 — Learning mechanics

This is what turns a site into a course.

- **A thirty-day path.** One screen per day, in the tradition's order: mechanism
  first, then ethics, then practice, then the fine grain. Each day ends with one
  question and one thing to notice before tomorrow. *(This may be what "more days"
  meant — a day-by-day programme rather than a reference.)*
- **Self-check per chapter.** Three questions, immediate feedback, no score kept
  beyond "seen it / got it."
- **Progress, kept locally.** Which boards opened, which simulators run, which
  chapters finished. Visible in the rail.
- **Spaced review of the term map.** Terms you have seen come back at
  lengthening intervals. Seventy-one terms is exactly the size where this works.
- **"One thing today."** A single term, quote or practice on the home page,
  rotating daily.

## 5. What makes it attractive — the rules already in force, plus three

The site's existing rules are why it works and they should not change: one
insight per screen; every abstraction gets an everyday example; every list shows
its generating structure; Sinhala on everything; interactive beats static; layers
labelled honestly; the metaphor audited.

Three more, for the phases above:

1. **Progression is visible.** People stay with things that show them moving. The
   fetter register, the gradual-training meter and the thirty-day path all exist
   to make progress *seen*.
2. **The source is one click away.** Every quoted passage links to the full
   discourse. Trust comes from being able to check.
3. **The life is told as an investigation.** Not a devotional biography — a
   person who tried the two available methods, found both failed, and worked out a
   third. The awakening night as three findings. That framing is both accurate and
   the most attractive version of the story.
